## 3. Voice and content patterns

Observed public caption style:

- Short, confident, visual: "One arm check", "Try to be different", "Rate this transition", "⏳…"
- Participation prompts: "Rate this from 1 to 10", "فنظاركم شكون ايربح ليوم؟" ("Who do you think will win today?")
- Training meme language: "Pov: you are the 0.1%"
- Bilingual and trilingual communication: English, French, Moroccan Arabic
- Generous use of emojis (🔥, 💪🏻, 🌙, ❤️, ⏳) and hashtags
- Music-led reels and collaboration posts
- Association pride and Moroccan location cues

### Voice implication for the website

Write in short, declarative lines. Keep claims concrete. The site should sound like an athlete speaking with quiet authority, not a generic supplement brand. Use English as the primary interface, with small touches of French/Arabic only where they are already part of the public identity and remain understandable. Do not overuse emojis on the website.

Proposed line quality:

- "Strength is built. Control is earned."
- "No shortcuts. Just the work."
- "The body is the first machine."
- "Marrakech made. Bodyweight only."

These are **creative copy proposals**, not quotes or verified claims.

## 4. Visual observations from public media

The visual world is more interesting than a standard dark gym template:

- Marrakech rooftops, cream/yellow walls, checkerboard tiled courtyards, deep blue sky.
- Improvised and street training environments: a stable yard, family courtyard, local gym, beach at dusk.
- Hard, natural side light; long shadows; practical environments rather than studio sets.
- The athlete's silhouette and precise body shapes are the strongest visual asset.
- Repeated motifs: hardened fingers on parallelttes, the perfect vertical handstand line, the body held parallel to the ground, shadow on tiles.
- The public image treatment is documentary, not polished sports campaign. The website can art-direct this into a premium editorial system without pretending the source images are high-end studio photography.

## 6. Strategic direction

### Positioning

**The body as architecture.**  
A Marrakech calisthenics athlete who trains with bodyweight, geometry, and control. Not a gym transformation brand. Not a generic online coach. The site should feel like a piece of sports editorial built around precision and place.

### Hero concept

A procedural steel monument: a large ring, pull-up uprights, a floating bar, a stone plinth, and drifting chalk dust. The object is abstract, athletic, and architectural. It is not a fake render of the athlete. The real photography carries the human proof.

### Signature element

**The Meridian:** the suspended ring and bar sculpture, used as a continuous coordinate system through the hero, the philosophy window, and the final call to action. Technical annotations, a moving section rail, and restrained lime accents turn the page into a training dossier rather than a landing page.

### Conversion

One primary action: **Enquire on Instagram**. No fake booking form, no invented pricing, no "apply now" flow that goes nowhere. Secondary action: explore the training sections.

### Tone

Ultra-premium, quiet, physical, measured. Few words. Strong type. Deliberate motion. No hype claims.
